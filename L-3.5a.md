## Context (fixed — do not re-litigate)
- Open item O-25, the fleet's last hole in budget truth: Leadfinder calls the Anthropic API, keeps its own USD ledger and enforces its own $100/day cap, but reports none of it to the Chief. The Chief's all-reporters total is therefore wrong by whatever Leadfinder spent, and a commanded discovery's real cost is invisible.
- Two apps have already done this and their pattern is proven: report per UTC day per vendor in $0.50 quanta, one request per quantum, `external_id` idempotent, `initiated_by` from the closed set, retry 5xx with the same `external_id`, never retry 4xx, a 4xx latches the reporter off loudly rather than looping. The Chief's inbound contract is `POST /api/ingest/spend` with a Bearer token; read the Chief's CONTRACT.md if reachable, otherwise mirror the shape here and state what you assumed.
- Leadfinder now runs on PostgreSQL (L-3.4g), so a cursor survives restarts. Its own ledger is the source figure; do not re-derive spend from anywhere else.
- Attribution matters: a job commanded by the Chief and a job a human started must not be reported the same way. Leadfinder already distinguishes chief-commanded jobs by their `external_id` and the machine surface they arrived on.

## Scope
1. Outbound spend reporter on the proven pattern: config `CHIEF_URL` + `CHIEF_INGEST_TOKEN`; unset means dormant with one loud line per boot and no crash. A cursor table (or column) records what has been reported per day per vendor, in the app's own migration mechanism, so a restart never double-reports and never loses a quantum.
2. Attribution: spend caused by a chief-commanded job reports `initiated_by: "chief"`; spend from a human job reports `initiated_by: "human"`. If the existing ledger cannot distinguish them per row today, say so plainly and report what it can prove rather than guessing — an honest partial attribution beats a confident wrong one.
3. Investigate one observation before deciding anything: on 2026-08-11 the Chief's card showed Leadfinder's `spend_today_usd` at $7.6492 all evening and then $0.0000 at 21:00 UTC. Determine from the code whether that figure is read from the durable ledger or from an in-process accumulator that a restart resets, and whether the day boundary is UTC or local. Fix it if it is a real defect; if the reading was legitimate, record why. Whatever the answer, the status endpoint's `spend_today_usd` and the reported quanta must agree in scope and in day boundary, and that agreement is a test.
4. Report in the ledger and on the status endpoint what remains unreported at any moment (the cursor's lag), so a silent reporter is visible rather than assumed.
5. Print the as-built reporting behaviour at the end of the report: which figure is reported, in what quanta, with which attribution, on what day boundary, and what the Chief will therefore show.
